Premium

A planner that solves for time

Give it a workload and your constraints. It returns a schedule that respects both — and tells you when they cannot both be respected.

How it works, step by step

1

Describe your workload

Type or paste it as you would say it out loud. No syntax, no forms. “Finish the maths assignment by Wednesday, study Java for two hours, presentation for Friday.”

2

Review what it extracted

Planora shows every task it found with the date, duration and priority it inferred — and the exact phrase it used as evidence. Edit anything before continuing.

3

Set your real constraints

Working hours per day, days you are unavailable, daily capacity, break habits, and any fixed commitments you already have.

4

Generate

The scheduler places work deadline-first, priority-second, into 15-minute slots, inserting breaks and splitting long tasks across days.

5

Adjust in a sentence

“Make Wednesday lighter.” “Move Java revision to Thursday.” “I only have two hours tomorrow.” The plan is rebuilt around the new constraint.

6

Accept

Accepted plans sync to your task list, calendar and dashboard. Nothing is marked complete just because it was scheduled.

What the planner optimises for

  • Deadline urgency, first and hardest
  • Priority within the same deadline window
  • Your real available hours, not theoretical ones
  • Fixed commitments already in your week
  • Break periods after sustained focus
  • Load spread across days instead of piled on one

What it will not do

It will not tell you that an impossible week is possible. If work cannot fit, you get the task name, the arithmetic, and the specific constraint to relax.

It will not invent tasks you did not describe, and it will not mark anything complete on your behalf.
